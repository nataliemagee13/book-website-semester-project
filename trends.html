BookTok Trend Tracker

This page pulls together the part of my project that looks at how BookTok has grown over time and what that means for the books people keep seeing online.

BookTok Keeps Growing

The Google Trends data I collected shows that search interest in “BookTok” has grown a lot from 2021 to 2026. In 2021, the search numbers were still pretty low, but the graph starts moving upward over time. By 2023 and 2024, BookTok was much more visible, and by 2025 and early 2026, the search interest was reaching some of its highest points.

This matters because it shows that BookTok is not just a random trend that disappeared quickly. It has kept growing over several years, which helps explain why publishers, bookstores, and readers are paying more attention to it.

The Biggest Change Is Visibility

One thing I noticed from the graph is that BookTok becomes much more visible after 2022. The trend line is not perfectly smooth, but it keeps moving upward overall. This shows that people were not just talking about one viral book for a short time. Instead, BookTok became a larger space where people kept searching for book recommendations, trending titles, and reading content.

This connects to my research question because it shows how social media can shape reading trends over time. The more visible BookTok becomes, the more influence it can have on what books people see and decide to read.

What the Graph Does Not Show

The graph is useful, but it does not tell the whole story. Google Trends can show that people are searching for BookTok more often, but it does not explain exactly why they are searching or what books they actually bought after searching. It also does not show every post, hashtag, or recommendation that helped a book become popular.

Even with those limits, the graph still helps my project because it gives me a way to track the rise of BookTok over time instead of only talking about it generally.

Social Media Keeps Books Alive Longer

Another thing this data made me think about is how social media can keep books popular for longer than they might have been before. A book does not have to be brand new to keep showing up online. If people keep recommending it, reacting to it, or making videos about it, the book can stay visible for years.

This helps explain books like Atomic Habits. It was published in 2018, but it continues to show up in online recommendation spaces because its ideas are easy to share in short posts and videos. I cannot prove that it would not have been popular without social media, but I can argue that social media helped extend its popularity and keep it part of the conversation.

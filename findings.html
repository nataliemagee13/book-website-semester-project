What I found

1. BookTok has grown over time

The Google Trends data shows that search interest in BookTok rises from 2021 to 2026. This supports my thesis because it shows that BookTok became more visible over time instead of disappearing as a short trend.

2. Genres do not trend the same way

The genre graphs showed that romance, romantasy, fantasy, thrillers, and self-help all have different search patterns. This helped me see that social media does not shape every genre in the same way.

3. Romantasy is the clearest genre shift

Romantasy was one of the strongest examples because search interest stayed low for years and then rose quickly around 2023 and 2024. This connects to BookTok because fantasy romance books and series became much more visible online.

4. Books have different popularity timelines

The book title graphs showed different types of popularity. Atomic Habits has repeated spikes, Fourth Wing spikes around release hype, The Housemaid rises later, It Ends With Us has one intense spike, and A Court of Thorns and Roses builds over time. This showed me that BookTok can affect books in different ways.

5. Women authors are especially visible

My women authors section helped me see that many authors connected to BookTok trends are women, especially in romance, romantasy, YA, and thrillers. This matters because BookTok is shaping not only which books get attention, but also which authors and genres become more visible.

Final takeaway:

Overall, my project supports the idea that social media shapes reading trends by changing visibility. It does not fully prove that BookTok caused every trend, but it shows that online attention, recommendation culture, and search interest are connected.

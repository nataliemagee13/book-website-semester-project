New Paths to Popularity

One thing my project keeps showing is that books do not have to become popular in the old way anymore. Before social media, a book's biggest moment was usually around release, bookstore placement, reviews, or publisher marketing. Now, a book can become popular later, stay visible longer, or reach readers because people keep recommending it online.

Before Social Media vs. After BookTok

Before Social Media

  • Publishers and bookstores had more control over visibility
  • Books usually got the most attention around release
  • Reviews and bestseller lists helped shape popularity
  • Older titles were less likely to suddenly come back

After BookTok

  • Readers can help create trends
  • Older books can get a second life
  • Authors can build audiences directly
  • A single book can become part of a larger genre or hashtag trend
  • Visibility can last longer through repeated recommendations

How Popularity Moves Now

1

A Book Appears

A title can show up through a new release, an older backlist title, or even a self-published book that starts getting noticed online.

2

Readers Repeat It

People post reactions, recommendations, edits, and quote videos, which keeps the book in front of more readers.

3

Popularity Stays Open-Ended

The book does not disappear after release. It can keep coming back through hashtags, fandoms, and search results.

Self-publishing and Direct Reader Attention

Social media also gives authors another way to reach readers. Self-published and less traditionally promoted authors can build attention through online communities, reviews, and recommendations. This does not mean social media is the only reason an author succeeds, but it does give readers more power in deciding which books get noticed.

One-off titles can stay popular too

Not every online book trend is part of a series. Some one-off titles stay popular because they are easy to recommend again and again. Atomic Habits is a good example of this because it was published in 2018, but it still shows up in recommendation spaces years later. Its ideas are easy to quote, summarize, and apply, which makes it work well in short videos, productivity posts, and online recommendation lists. My Google Trends graph also showed repeated spikes instead of one short viral moment, which supports the idea that social media can help extend a book's popularity over time.

What this changes

Older Books Can Come Back

A book does not have to be new to trend. If readers keep posting about it, the book can become visible again.

Authors Can Reach Readers Directly

Social media gives authors and readers a space to connect outside of only traditional publisher marketing.

Popularity Lasts Longer

A book can stay in the conversation if people keep turning it into quotes, reactions, reviews, and recommendations.

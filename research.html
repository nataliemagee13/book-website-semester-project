Research & Findings

My project looks at how social media shapes reading trends through BookTok, Goodreads, Google Trends data, and online recommendation culture. This page combines the main research, process, findings, and conclusion so the research side of the project is easier to follow.

What I’m researching

My research question is: How does social media shape reading trends? My project looks at how BookTok, Goodreads, Google Trends data, and online recommendation culture influence which books, genres, and authors become more visible over time.

Social media shapes reading trends by making certain books, genres, and authors more visible. BookTok does not just recommend books. It can change the timeline of popularity by helping older books come back, pushing genres like romantasy and thrillers, and giving women authors more visibility through reader-driven content.

What the research says

1

BookTok works like a recommendation system

The research shows that BookTok is more than people casually talking about books. Readers post reviews, reactions, rankings, and short videos that make certain titles more visible.

Low, Ehret, and Hagh · Dera

2

Algorithms help shape visibility

Platforms like TikTok and Goodreads do not just show random recommendations. Their algorithms and rating systems influence what readers see, which affects what books gain attention.

Murray · Goodreads

3

Reading has become more public

Reading is not only private anymore. People now post reactions, reviews, quotes, rankings, and opinions online, which turns reading into a more social experience.

Steiner · Asplund

4

BookTok affects the book industry too

BookTok trends now show up in bookstores, publisher marketing, and sales conversations. This shows that online attention can move beyond social media and affect the actual book market.

Barnes & Noble · Publishers Weekly

How I researched it

For this project, I used a mix of research sources, Google Trends data, and website design. I looked at academic articles about BookTok, Goodreads, algorithms, and digital reading culture. I also used industry sources like Barnes & Noble, TikTok, Publishers Weekly, and Goodreads to see how BookTok shows up outside of academic research.

1

Collected sources

I gathered academic and industry sources about BookTok, Goodreads, authors, and online reading culture.

2

Tracked search interest

I used Google Trends to look at BookTok, popular genres, and specific book titles from 2021 to 2026.

3

Compared patterns

I looked at whether books and genres had slow growth, sudden spikes, repeated spikes, or long-term visibility.

4

Built the website

I used HTML, CSS, VS Code, GitHub, Copilot, and ChatGPT to organize the project into a visual website.

What I found

1

BookTok grew over time

My Google Trends data showed that search interest in BookTok increased from 2021 to 2026. This supports my thesis because it shows that BookTok became more visible over time instead of disappearing as a short trend.

2

Genres do not trend the same way

Romance, romantasy, fantasy, thrillers, and self-help all showed different search patterns. This helped me see that social media does not shape every genre in the same way.

3

Romantasy is the clearest genre shift

Romantasy stayed low for years and then rose quickly around 2023 and 2024. This connects to BookTok because fantasy romance books became much more visible online.

4

Books have different popularity timelines

Some books spike quickly, some build slowly, and some stay visible for years. This shows that BookTok can change the normal timeline of popularity.

5

Women authors are especially visible

Many authors connected to BookTok trends are women, especially in romance, romantasy, YA, and thrillers. This shows that social media affects not only which books trend, but also which authors get attention.

6

Social media creates second chances

A book does not have to be new to become visible. Social media can bring older books back, extend their popularity, or create new attention around a genre, series, or author.

What the data cannot prove

Google Trends is useful because it shows search interest over time, but it does not prove exactly why someone searched for a book or whether they actually read it. A spike could come from BookTok, a movie adaptation, publisher marketing, controversy, or multiple things happening at once. Because of that, I used Google Trends as evidence of visibility and attention, not as perfect proof of cause and effect.

What this all shows

Overall, my project shows that social media shapes reading trends by changing visibility. BookTok, Goodreads, and online recommendation culture influence which books people see, which genres grow, and which authors stay in the conversation. My data does not prove that BookTok caused every trend, but it does show that online attention and reading trends are connected.

The biggest thing I learned is that books do not become popular in only one way anymore. Social media can create quick spikes, long-term visibility, second chances for older books, and major genre trends.
